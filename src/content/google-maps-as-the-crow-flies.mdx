Google Maps gives driving, walking and transit distances by default. To get the distance **as the crow flies**, the straight line between two points, you need its **Measure distance** tool. It works in a web browser on a computer and in the Google Maps app on Android and iPhone.

Or skip the steps: type two places into the calculator above and it gives the straight line distance, the direction and the map in one go.

## Measure as the crow flies on Google Maps on a computer

<MeasureSteps />

<div class="steps">

1. Open Google Maps in a web browser and find your starting point. Zoom in so you can place it accurately.
2. **Right-click** the starting point. Right-click on a blank part of the map rather than on a place label, or you may get that place's own menu.
3. Choose **Measure distance** at the bottom of the menu. A small circle marks the start.
4. **Click** the destination. A straight line joins the two points, and the distance appears both beside the line and in a box at the bottom of the screen.
5. To measure a path with turns, keep clicking to add more points. The box shows the total of all the segments.
6. Drag any point to move it, or click a point to remove it. To finish, close the box at the bottom of the screen, or right-click the map and choose **Clear measurement**.

</div>

If you click back on the first point to close the shape, Google Maps on a computer also shows the area inside it, which is handy for measuring a field, a lot or a roof.

<Callout title="Using a Mac trackpad">
On a Mac, right-click is a two-finger click on the trackpad, or Control-click. On a Chromebook, tap the touchpad with two fingers or press Alt while you click.
</Callout>

## Measure as the crow flies on Android and iPhone

The Google Maps app has the same tool, but it works by moving the map under a crosshair rather than by clicking.

<div class="steps">

1. Open the Google Maps app.
2. **Touch and hold** the starting point on the map, away from any place name or icon. A red pin drops.
3. Tap the dropped pin's name at the bottom of the screen, or swipe up on the panel, to open its details.
4. Tap **Measure distance**. A black circle, the crosshair, appears in the middle of the map.
5. **Move the map** until the crosshair sits on the next point. The distance at the bottom updates as you move.
6. To measure a route with turns, tap **Add point** and move the map to the next point. Repeat as needed.
7. To take back the last point, tap **Undo**. To start over, open the menu and choose **Clear**.

</div>

The app shows distance, but it does not show area. If the **Measure distance** option is missing, make sure you have dropped a pin on an unlabeled spot rather than tapped a business or a landmark, and that the app is up to date.

## Why use Measure distance instead of directions?

Directions give the length of a route along roads, paths or transit lines. Measure distance gives the shortest line over the ground. The two can be very different.

A house listed as "two miles from the station" might be two miles in a straight line and three by road. Measure distance tells you the first; Directions tells you the second. Our [distance between two addresses](/distance-between-two-addresses/) calculator shows both side by side.

## Why the line curves on long measurements

Measure a short distance and the line is straight. Measure from New York to London and it bends north in an arc. The line is still the shortest path; it only looks curved because a flat map has to stretch the round Earth.

The shortest route over a globe is a great circle. Drawn on a flat world map, a great circle between two places in the northern hemisphere bows toward the North Pole. That is why long flights head far north, over Canada, Greenland or the Arctic, on routes that look roundabout on a flat map.

## Other ways to measure straight line distance

### Google Earth

Google Earth has a **ruler** tool. In Google Earth on the web, choose the ruler icon in the menu on the left, click the start, then click the end. In Google Earth Pro on a computer, choose **Tools**, then **Ruler**, and pick a **Line** or a **Path**. Google Earth shows the Earth as a globe, so long lines look as they really are.

### Apple Maps

Apple Maps does not have a built-in tool for measuring a straight line between two points at the time of writing. Directions in Apple Maps give route distances only. Use a calculator, Google Maps or Google Earth instead.

## Tips for an accurate measurement

- **Zoom in at both ends.** At city scale, a point dropped a little off can change a short distance noticeably. Zoom in on the start, place it, then zoom in on the end.
- **Measure from the right spot.** A building's front door, a property line or a trailhead may be some way from where its map label sits.
- **Remember it ignores height.** The measurement is the horizontal distance. Climbing a mountain covers more ground than the map line shows.
- **Check the units.** Google Maps shows miles or kilometers depending on your region. Our calculator shows both, plus nautical miles.

## Common problems

- **"Measure distance" isn't in the menu.** On a computer, right-click a blank spot rather than a place label. On a phone, drop a pin on an unlabeled spot first. Google's [help page](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/1628031) notes that the tool does not work when Google Maps is in Lite mode.
- **The line jumps when you try to pan.** On a computer, a click adds a point. Drag the map to pan instead of clicking.
- **The phone app shows no total.** Make sure you started from a dropped pin and tapped Measure distance; the total appears at the bottom only while the crosshair is showing.
