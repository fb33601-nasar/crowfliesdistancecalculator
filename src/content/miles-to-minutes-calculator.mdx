How many minutes a number of miles takes depends on one thing: your average speed. At 60 mph a mile takes one minute. At 30 mph it takes two. Walking at 3 mph it takes 20.

The calculator above opens on 10 miles, about 15 to 20 minutes by car at the 30–40 mph that is typical for a drive that long.

## How to use the miles to minutes calculator

<div class="steps">

1. Leave **Distance → time** selected and type a distance in miles or kilometers, or press a preset.
2. Read the estimate for a drive of that length at typical average speeds, then the full table of times from walking to an open interstate.
3. If you know your speed, type it in **Your own speed** and it is added to the table, highlighted.
4. To go the other way, choose **Time → distance**, enter a number of minutes, and see how far you can travel in that time at each speed.

</div>

## The formula

<div class="formula">

minutes = miles ÷ speed in mph × 60

</div>

For 25 miles at 40 mph: 25 ÷ 40 = 0.625 hours, and 0.625 × 60 = 37.5 minutes. The same formula works in kilometers if the speed is in km/h.

To go the other way, from minutes to miles:

<div class="formula">

miles = minutes ÷ 60 × speed in mph

</div>

In 45 minutes at 50 mph: 45 ÷ 60 = 0.75 hours, and 0.75 × 50 = 37.5 miles.

## What average speed should you use?

The speed limit is not your average speed. Traffic lights, junctions, turns, slow traffic and the first and last miles all pull the average down, and the effect is strongest on short trips.

<SampleSpeeds />

In our sample, drives across a city center, such as the Empire State Building to One World Trade Center, averaged 14 to 26 mph even with no traffic modeled. Trips of 40 to 100 miles averaged 43 to 52 mph, and long highway trips of 170 miles or more averaged 45 to 58 mph. Real traffic would bring all of these down further.
